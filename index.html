About Me

I am an AI researcher working on reliable multimodal agents: systems that can perceive visual environments, reason over time, and take grounded actions. My work spans vision-language models, LLM reasoning and planning, and multimodal evaluation, with a focus on visual grounding, temporal video understanding, and reducing hallucinations.

At the University of Maryland and UMBC, I have worked on VLM hallucination mitigation, trace-grounded video reasoning, executable planning from natural language, code reasoning, and persona-aware conversational agents. I also have applied engineering experience building multimodal RAG systems and agentic workflow integrations.

My long-term goal is to develop agents that combine vision, language, memory, and planning to complete reliable long-horizon tasks — first in digital environments and eventually in embodied settings.

Research Interests

Multimodal perception and grounding: Vision-language models, video understanding, temporal reasoning, visual grounding, and hallucination detection and mitigation.

Reasoning, planning, and verification: LLM agents, state tracking, executable planning, code reasoning, and methods that let agents check and correct their own outputs.

Memory, world models, and action: Long-horizon multimodal memory, world models, visual-context compression, vision-language-action models, and agents for GUIs and embodied environments.

Broadly, I am interested in AI systems that know when to look, what to remember, how to reason, when to verify, and how to act.
